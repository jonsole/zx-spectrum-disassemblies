Room structure

Alien 8's cryogenic ship is 128 rooms on Knight Lore's 16 by 16 grid, each a record in the room directory at ROOM02. This page draws every one of them with the game's own code, puts them together into one map in the game's own projection, marks on it where the game's things are, and gives each room's record decoded. The grid, the record and the builder are Knight Lore's (its castle); Pentagram, the next game on the engine, gave the grid up (its world).

What is measured and what is read. Measured, in SkoolKit's simulator: the pictures (each room built and drawn by the game, and read out of its screen buffer); what each room holds as built (the sockets, the robots, lifts and blocks, the things on the ceiling, and each place's valve or life, which were checked against the records); the deadly things (from the bits the game sets, after a turn); what the places held in the simulator's game; and the doorways (each one walked through). Read from the game's tables and code: the record format, the sizes, which background is a doorway and in which wall, where each doorway leads, the start rooms, the places' positions, and what the latch on the falling things and the dealing of the places do. The map's layout is the game's own arithmetic on room numbers.

The station

A room's number is its square on a 16 by 16 grid: the high four bits the row, the low four the column. Leaving by a doorway changes the number by arithmetic alone, in the robot's move (HANDLE_EXIT_SCREEN): out through the wall at higher U adds 1 and at lower U takes 1, changing only the column so that it wraps within the row (EXIT_HIGH_U, EXIT_LOW_U); out at higher V adds 16 and at lower V takes 16 (EXIT_HIGH_V, EXIT_LOW_V). On the screen +U runs down to the right and +V up to the right (CALC_PIXEL_XY); here, as on Knight Lore's page, +U is called east and +V north. Whichever wall he leaves by, the exit leaves a marker of 0 or $FF in his U or V, and ADJUST_PLYR_UVZ_FOR_ROOM_SIZE turns it into a place just inside the opposite wall of the next room, at the height of the arch there (ADJUST_PLYR_Z_FOR_ARCH).

128 of the 256 squares are rooms, in rows 0 to 15 and columns 0 to 15. Below, every room is drawn by the game and put where the game's own projection places its square, on its floor; the back rooms are pasted first, so nearer rooms stand in front. Click a room for its entry, or see the whole station at full size. The markers show where things are, each from the game's own tables or from the rooms as the game builds them; tick a box to show its layer.

The doorways

A doorway is a background whose first piece is a pillar of graphic 2, whose update routine (FIRST_PILLAR) lets the robot through the wall while he stands in the arch. Which wall it is in follows from where the pillar stands: backgrounds 0 north; 1 east; 2 south; 3 west; 10 east (raised); 11 south (raised). The builder takes a room's doorways to be its first four backgrounds (ADJUST_PLYR_Z_FOR_ARCH looks only there for the arch he comes in by), and in every room they are.

There are 300 doorways. Every one leads to a room that exists and has a doorway in the opposite wall leading back (300 of 300), and none crosses the edge of the grid, so the wrapping arithmetic is never used. 273 are at floor level and 27 are raised: their arch stands 48 units up, on a ledge of two blocks (backgrounds 12 and 13), and all of them are in the east and south walls, the near ones the picture does not draw. Every raised doorway's partner on the other side is at floor level (27 of 27).

Every doorway was tried in the simulator when this page was built: the room built, the robot stood 12 units inside the wall in the middle of the arch (or, where something stands there, at the nearest clear spot further in), facing out, and the walk key held, with any death taken back at each turn so that what is tested is whether the way is open; where objects cover the floor in front of the arch, he was also stood on top of them. 259 of the 273 floor-level doorways let him straight through; 13 more only with jump held as well (room $14's east doorway, room $14's west doorway, room $54's north doorway, room $54's south doorway, room $57's north doorway, room $57's south doorway, room $6B's north doorway, room $74's east doorway, room $87's east doorway, room $87's north doorway, room $87's south doorway, room $87's west doorway, room $A7's south doorway); 1 only from on top of what covers the floor in front of the arch ($4A west); none is blocked. Standing on the ledge of a raised doorway and walking out took him through 27 of the 27; walking and jumping at one from the floor below took him through 0. In all 300, he came out in the room the arithmetic names.

The raised doorways matter. Taking only the floor-level doorways he walked through, the station falls into 13 regions, each a set of rooms that can all reach one another: one of 88 rooms, which holds 3 of the 4 start rooms and 14 of the 24 chambers, and 12 more, of 1 to 10 rooms each. Between two regions the pair of doorways always has one raised and one at floor level, so every crossing is a walk one way and a climb onto a ledge 48 units up the other. A walk and a jump from the floor did not get him onto a ledge in any of the 27 rooms with one (above); how he does it in play -- by climbing what the room holds -- this page does not try to settle. A room record

The records are packed end to end at ROOM02, with no index: BUILD_ROOM finds a room by stepping from one record to the next until the number matches. In order:

ByteWhat
0The room's number: row in bits 4-7, column in bits 0-3.
1How many bytes follow: the distance from this byte to the next record. The builder counts them down and stops wherever they run out -- even partway through a group of objects (no room's record does).
2Bits 6-7 the size, an index into ROOM_SIZES; bits 3-5 the room's colour; bits 0-2 the ink it is drawn in, which is clear on the tape: RESET_ROOM_COLOURS copies bits 3-5 into them at every new game, and activating the room's chamber makes them white (LOOSE_VALVE).
3 ...The backgrounds, a byte each: an index into BACKGROUND_TABLE (see the scenery). Each background is a list of eight-byte pieces, a record each, which carry their own position.
$FFThe end of the backgrounds, if objects follow. A room with none just ends.
groupsA byte whose bits 3-7 are an object template, an index into OBJECT_TABLE (see the templates), and bits 0-2 the number of copies less one; then a position byte for each copy. Two template numbers are not templates: 0 takes the byte after it as a placement nudge for the groups that follow (41 rooms use it), and 31 moves on to the second page of templates, indexes 32 to 39, for the rest of the record (12 rooms), and skips the byte after it. Both cost two bytes whatever their count says.

A position byte is a cell of the floor and a level: bits 0-2 the U cell and bits 3-5 the V cell of an 8 by 8 grid, bits 6-7 the level. The builder makes U = 72 + 16 times the U cell, V likewise, and Z = the floor + 12 times the level. The nudge adds 8 to U if its bit 0 is set, 8 to V if bit 1 is, and its bits 2-7 to Z. The values the rooms use: $30 41 times; $00 1 time ($44). $30 lifts the objects after it 48 units, onto a ledge or a stack.
