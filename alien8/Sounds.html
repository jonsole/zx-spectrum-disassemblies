Sounds: the effects and the tunes

A 48K Spectrum has one bit of sound hardware, bit 4 of port $FE, and Alien 8 makes every sound by setting and clearing it with the processor counting in between, the border kept black. While a sound plays nothing else happens: the game stands still for a tune, and every beep lengthens the turn it is in. There are two players. The tunes go through PLAY_NOTE, a note at a time; everything else is waves from CLICK, which turns the speaker on for B turns of a 13 T-state loop and off for as long, and BEEP ($B6FB, the end of FOOTSTEP), which repeats it C times. Most of the effects are Knight Lore's routines, some of them with new callers; a few are Alien 8's own (the robots' warble, WARBLE_SOUND).

The tune player and its note table are Knight Lore's, and Pentagram's (see Knight Lore's sounds and Pentagram's). A tune is a string of note bytes ended by $FF: the low six bits index NOTES, five octaves of semitones from G#1 to G6 with 0 a rest, and the top two bits are the length less one, one to four units of about 0.155 s. Against Knight Lore's snapshot at this build: of the 88 bytes of PLAY_NOTE, the 4 that differ from Knight Lore's player ($B2DA) are exactly the two addresses in it, the CALL to ADD_HL_A and the LD of NOTES; and the 183 bytes of NOTES are its note table ($B332) exactly. Against Pentagram's snapshot at this build: of the 88 bytes of PLAY_NOTE, the 4 that differ from Pentagram's player ($D6C0) are exactly the two addresses in it, the CALL to ADD_HL_A and the LD of NOTES; and the 183 bytes of NOTES are its note table ($D718) exactly.

Every recording here is the game's own code running in SkoolKit's simulator, each change of the speaker bit logged to the T-state and rendered at 44100 Hz; nothing is synthesised. Most effects were recorded from the game playing -- the very runs the Animations page shows -- so each is heard as it is in the game, with whatever else sounded in those turns, and each is checked wave by wave against what the code and the objects' records say it should be: every wave's half-wave count, and every off half-wave inside a sound against the instruction timings of the loop that timed it. The simulator has no memory contention, so all of this runs a touch faster, and a touch higher, than on a real Spectrum, where the ULA holds up the OUTs. A quiet turn (the empty room, $6D, nothing moving) lasts 121,037 T-states, 35 ms; the calls that stand for a sound made over several turns are spaced by that.

The robot

Walking

FOOTSTEP, from HANDLE_FORWARD each time the legs leave a stride (frames 0 and 2 of four): a footstep every other turn, its pitch a fixed half-wave count of 96 or, on alternate pairs of turns (bit 1 of the turn counter), one from his height -- a tick and a tock -- and its length, (U/2 + (256-V)/2)/16 waves, longer towards plus U and shorter towards plus V. Knight Lore's footstep.

Recorded: the game running in the empty room ($6D): walk held for 16 turns from standing, facing plus V, and two turns after.

Checked: 61 waves, 122 edges (the code implies 61 and 122); every on half-wave was 13B + 18 T-states for the B the code gives it; and every off half-wave inside a sound was 13B plus 64 (BEEP's, in FOOTSTEP) -- the loops' own timing.

747 ms, 122 edges, 18 turns, 1358 to 2035 Hz.

Turning

The turn's sound: TURNING_LEGS, as a part-way view ends and the legs take the new facing, calls FOOTSTEP past its test on the frame (FOOTSTEP_NOW, $B6D4): a footstep, whatever the legs' frame. So a quarter turn ends in a tick. TURNING_LEGS does not keep BC round the call, and the footstep's loop leaves C, the controls, at zero: on that turn he takes no step and gravity ignores a held jump.

Recorded: the game running: the turning animation's run -- right turn held from standing for a whole circle, four quarter turns.
