The planet: the whole world map

Cyberun's planet is a ring of 96 by 28 map cells, each a page the size of the play area, 224 pixels by 160. landscape_map holds a tile number for each (row 0, the sky, is not stored), and tile_table turns it into a tile record (see the tiles). 160 of the 171 tiles are used; 4 have no pieces at all ($00, $20, $2A, $A6), and 1843 of the 2688 cells hold one of those: sky, solid rock and the inside of a hill are the same empty tile, told apart only by what is on the screen already. The surface's lowest row is 11, the caverns run from row 12 down, and rows 1 to 3 hold no pieces at all. Every planet is this one map; only the two landscape colours (planet_ground_colours) and the name change.

How this picture was made

Every cell is drawn by the game's own landscape code, draw_landscape, run in SkoolKit's simulator one pass at a time through scroll_landscape, with the stack where the play loop has it. The game never clears its screen: the sky is black and the ground's inside is the checker $55/$AA, both unchanged by a 2-pixel move, so the renderer draws only each piece's steps and paints only the strips a moving edge uncovers. A cell drawn once from a black screen therefore shows just the outline and the lips of its pieces (left, below). The game gets a whole picture by its fly-in (start_landscape): the play area filled first, then the view moved a page so that the fills paint behind every moving piece (fill_behind_moving_piece). This page does the same over the whole depth of the planet: for each map column the play area starts black, the view stands in row 2 (the sky), and it descends 4 lines a pass -- the fly-in's step -- to the bottom of the map. Every piece then comes up from the bottom of the play area, and the game's fills paint the checker under each floor and black under each ceiling as it rises. Whenever the view lies exactly on a cell, the play area is read. Nothing in the picture is filled or drawn by this page; the only start it is given is black, which is what the sky above row 3 is.

Checked: the game's own screen at the first pass of a new game, after its own fly-in, is the window of this picture at the view's place to 100.00% of its pixels (right, below).

The colours are the game's too, and carry a history in the same way. colour_piece colours the attribute cells along each piece's steps, magenta above row 12 and yellow from it down on the first planet; a cell no piece passes keeps the colour it had, so where a shaft or a stretch of rock goes down from the surface without pieces, the surface's colour comes down with it -- as it does on the screen when the ship flies down there. (A life started in the caverns begins with the whole play area in the caverns' colour.) During the fly-in tile $3E is drawn as tile $AA (load_map_window); the descent here is play, so it is drawn as itself.

The planet

Click a cell for its tile, or an empty one for its map row's listing; or see the whole planet at half size (10752 by 2240, each 2 by 2 block of pixels averaged, so the checker shows as an even half-tone). West is left; the world wraps from column $5F back to column $00. Tick a box to show a layer, each read from the game's tables.

Where a game starts: Measured: the ship's place at the first pass of play of a new game in the simulator, page column $0E at x $08, page row 7 at y $93. A new game's player records get their restart point from start_position (clear_player_record): the words $070D, $B790, $4078 -- map column $0D and row 7, the in-page x and y, and the scroll origins. start_landscape starts the view a row lower, in row 8, over a play area filled with the checker, and flies it up until the ship's place is clear sky (fly_in_step); the first pass of play found the view's page at column $0D, row 7, its x $90 and y $3B at the play area's edges. Every life after the first starts from the last safe place save_restart_point kept.

The rocket's place: Page column $52 at x $68, page row 10 at line $73: the rocket's base, the words rocket_x and rocket_y, from which draw_rocket draws the parts delivered so far and over which drop_part lets a carried part go. Every planet has its rocket here.

Pick-up places, group 1: 16 places at $609B; the dealing list deals places 0, 6, 8, 9 from it: add-on 1, the hook; add-on 7, the special beam (a post); add-on 9, the figure; add-on 10, the slab. See the pick-up places.

Pick-up places, group 2: 8 places at $60E3 (2 never dealt: deal_places marks them taken before it starts); the dealing list deals places 5, 7 from it: add-on 6, the fuel saver (a mound); add-on 8, the missiles (a bar). See the pick-up places.

Pick-up places, group 3: 16 places at place_pool_kind3; the dealing list deals places 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17 from it: rocket part 0, a section of bars; rocket part 1, a checked section; rocket part 2, a crate-like section; rocket part 3, a crate-like section; rocket part 4, a fin; rocket part 5, a fin; rocket part 6, the ore tank. See the pick-up places.

Pick-up places, group 4: 4 places at $6103; the dealing list deals place 18 from it: rocket part 7, the nose cone. See the pick-up places.

Pick-up places, group 5: 2 places at $60DB; the dealing list deals places 3, 4 from it: add-on 4, the side jet (a block); add-on 5, the lift jet (arches). See the pick-up places.

Refuel pads: 18 of them, kind $0E (refuel pad): animated graphic $1B in frames 4 and 5, which ship_hits_ground_objects treats as a refuel pad -- the tank filled, the ship repaired, and the pad used up for this player.

Ore deposits: 24 deposits, kind $08 (ore deposit), kind $09 (ore deposit), kind $0A (ore deposit): the kinds beam_hits_ground_object lets the special beam mine, one unit of ore each from kinds 8 and 9 and two from $0A (load_ore), 34 units in the world for a tank of 18.

Beam generators: 36 of them, kind $06 (beam generator), kind $07 (beam generator). Destroying one with the special beam sets the bit map_square_bit gives its page row and half of the map, which is the bit draw_beams tests before drawing a beam in a screen of the same row and half: so one generator switches off every beam in its row and half of the world.

Flickering-beam screens (tile $A6): 76 map cells of tile $A6, in rows 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9. draw_beams draws a one-line beam across each such screen in view, flickering in a random pattern and colour, and a beam touching the ship damages it (beam_hits_ship) -- the planet's only danger that is part of the map. Taking each beam screen's row and half: 18 of the 18 have a generator in the same row and half.

Things that throw what hurts: 99 of them, kind $03 (spiky mound), kind $04 (lamp), kind $05 (domed robot), kind $0D (pylon craft): every kind whose shot (bits 7-5 of its byte in object_kind_flags) is thrown as a frame of type $1C whose contact effect (small_object_effects) has the damage bit. ground_object_fires lets each fire one pass in 16 at random while it is on the screen. The things themselves never harm the ship: it bounces off them.

Things that throw the fuel thief: 10 of them, kind $0C (domed robot): the throw is the fuel thief, whose touch takes 16 steps of fuel.

Things that throw fuel: 12 of them, kind $00 (flask): the throw is the refuel pick-up, 28 steps of fuel.

Everything else lying on the planet: 91 more, kind $01 (pedestal), kind $02 (cloud), kind $0B (ringed planet), kind $0F (saucer), kind $10 (lamp): scenery that throws nothing and is not one of the above. The special beam can destroy any thing lying on the planet except kind $0B.
