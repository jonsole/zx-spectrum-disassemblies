Sounds: the effects and the tunes

A 48K Spectrum has one bit of sound hardware, bit 4 of port $FE, and Cyberun makes every sound by setting and clearing it with the processor counting in between, OUT ($FE) with $10 and then 0 so that the border stays black. Interrupts are off throughout the game, so nothing plays a sound over time: each effect plays in full, at once, inside the pass that asks for it, and that pass is longer by the sound's length. Two building blocks make every effect: a pulse with separate on and off lengths (beep_pulse) and a square-wave cycle (beep_cycle), driven by a rising sweep (rising_sweep), a falling zap (zap_sweep), a two-note warble (warble) and a scrambled crackle (crackle). The tunes are a byte a note, played by play_note from the semitones of note_table.

Every recording here is the game's own code running in SkoolKit's simulator, each change of the speaker bit logged to the T-state and rendered at 44100 Hz; nothing is synthesised. Each effect was recorded by calling its routine on a machine of its own -- the snapshot run through its start-up to the menu, then the registers and bytes the routine reads set -- and checked wave by wave against what its code makes: the half-wave counts it works out from its inputs, turned into T-states by the instruction timings of its loops. An effect played once a pass is called once for each of the inputs a run of passes gives it, the calls 205,844 T-states (59 ms) apart: the median length of a pass with no sound, measured with the ship still in the sky.

The simulator has no memory contention. The effects' code ($71D3-$731E) lies below $8000, in the RAM the ULA holds up while it draws the screen, so on a real 48K the effects run somewhat slower, and lower, than they are heard here; the tune player and its table lie above $8000 and are not slowed.

Tunes

A note is one byte: bits 0-5 its entry in note_table (0 a rest), bits 6-7 its length less one, one to four units. An entry gives the half-period as a DJNZ count B and a pass count C, and the cycles in one unit; play_note multiplies the cycles by the length and toggles the speaker, off then on, for each. Each tune below was recorded by calling its player with DE at the tune and no key down, so that the title tune plays to its end, as it does for a player who waits.

How the recorded pitches compare with the table. Every note the five tunes play was measured from its recording, as the mean of its whole waves, and every one is the pitch the note table gives it through play_note's loops -- a half period of 13B + 3339C - 3333 T-states, plus 39 for the off half and 62 for the on -- to within 0.000 Hz, the rounding of the mean; the half-waves themselves match to the T-state (the Recorded column above). The table runs from 52.6 Hz (entry 1) to 1524 Hz (entry 60). Set against an equal-tempered scale with A4 at 440 Hz it is nearest with entry 1 as G#1 and entry 60 as G6, its notes lying within 53 cents of that scale, and the notes the tunes play within 15 (the table below). It is not quite a scale of semitones as played: counted in loops, as 256(C - 1) + B turns of the DJNZ, its entries step by a semitone to within 7 cents (the rounding of B), but each half-wave also spends a fixed hundred-odd T-states outside its loops, which is a larger share of a short period than a long one, so the high notes come out flatter than the low: from the semitone series that fits the played pitches best, entry 1 lies +37 cents and entry 60 -34 (the table looks to have been worked out as loop counts -- an inference from these numbers). Entry 18 repeats the B and C of the entry before, so note 18 sounds as note 17 (only its cycle count differs) and the step from 17 to 19 is two semitones; no tune plays it.
