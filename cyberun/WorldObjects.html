The objects lying in the world

290 things lie at fixed places on the planet, in three tables sorted by map column: a kind byte each (map_object_kinds), a count for each column (map_objects_per_column, with a 97th byte repeating column 0's, 2, for the wrap from column $5F) and two packed position bytes each (map_object_places). Decoded here as spawn_objects_from_list unpacks them: the column from the running count, the map row from bits 0-4 of the second byte, the x in the page from bits 0-1 of the first (its bits 3-4) and bits 5-7 of the second, and the y (the bottom line) as bits 2-7 of the first less one. The counts add up to 290, and every place lies in its page: x 8 to 208 in steps of 8, y 39 to 183 in steps of 4, rows 1 to 28.

Whenever the view crosses into a new cell, load_map_window frees the slots whose things have left the 2 by 2 window and spawn_column_objects brings in the things of the window's two columns and rows, at most four at a time (ground_objects). A kind byte's bits 7 and 6 mark the thing gone for player 1 and player 2 (ship_hits_ground_objects sets one when a refuel pad is used up, beam_hits_ground_object when the special beam destroys one), and mask_world_objects clears them at a new game and the current player's at the end of a planet (planet_secured).

Each kind's byte in object_kind_flags gives bits 7-5 the kind of shot it throws (ground_object_fires, one pass in 16 at random while it is on the screen) and, with bit 4 set, makes it an animated graphic $1B whose two frames come from object_part_pairs; otherwise it is graphic $0D with the kind as its frame. Its colours are its byte in object_kind_bytes, one in each nibble, shown on alternate passes. The pictures are drawn by the game: the one thing brought back, moved within its column to the middle of a page in an emptied scratch copy of the map, brought in by load_map_window and drawn by update_ground_objects over a black play area, for 24 passes; every shape it took is shown. The bottom character row of some keeps the colour of the play area under it (trim_colour_patch stops the colour patch short so as not to colour the ground). The names are by appearance, or by what the code does with the kind.
