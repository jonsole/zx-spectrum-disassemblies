6D92: The landscape tiles' records
Each tile is drawn by draw_landscape as a list of pieces of ground, each a line of steps of one piece graphic. A record:
Up to three optional markers first. $99: upside down -- floors become ceilings: each piece's direction is swapped through the second half of the table at mirror_table (0 and 7, 1 and 8 ... 6 and 13 swap places, 14 and 15 swap), its y is turned over (XOR $0C) and its extra bytes XOR 1. $FF: mirrored left-right -- each piece's x is reflected, its two extra bytes are swapped (XOR 2) and its direction turned round through the table's first half. Then any byte of $95 or more is padding, skipped. Then the number of pieces; a record that is just 0 has none.
Each piece: its x and y in the page; a byte with the direction in bits 4-7 and the number of steps in bits 0-3 (0 means 16); and, only for directions 0 and 7, two extra bytes (piece_extra_a, piece_extra_b). The direction picks three bytes from piece_shapes by set_piece_shape: the step in x, the step in y, and a third that is 0 for directions 0 and 7 (the flat pieces) and 8 or 16 for the others. Directions 7 to 13 are the ceiling shapes of 0 to 6 and use the entry seven less, with piece_ceiling set.
The padding rule is what lets four tiles share a record: a plain record of n pieces at A can be preceded by $99 (A-1, upside down), $FF $99 (A-2, mirrored left-right: the $99 is padding) and $99 $FF $99 (A-3, both). So 72 records, one after another with nothing between, serve all 171 tiles: 74 tiles have no marker, 27 are upside down, 51 mirrored left-right and 19 both. Read in Python, every byte from here to the tune is used except one (an unused $99, the third byte of the block).
Laid out below a line per record, from the game's bytes when the disassembly is built: each marker a byte of its own, so that every tile's word in tile_table lands on one, then the count and the pieces; the comment names the tiles that read the record, and how, and decodes each piece as its (x,y) in the page, its direction and its steps, and a flat piece's two end codes.
