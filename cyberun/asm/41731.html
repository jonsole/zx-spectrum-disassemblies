A303: Rub out an object's old image and draw its new one, without flicker
Used by the routines at draw_object_images and draw_star_layer.
The sprite compositor, which draws every ground object and sky object, and the stars (draw_star_layer): about a third of all the game's time goes through here. Both images are worked on in a buffer, never on the screen, and the screen is written once, so nothing flickers. The steps:
1. The box: the smallest rectangle, whole bytes wide, covering both images: its left edge (piece_x) and bottom line (piece_y), and its width in bytes (at most 7) and height plus one, kept in piece_dx and piece_rise. If only one image is present, the other's position is taken as its own.
2. Copy the box from the screen into the buffer (copy_box_in), bottom row first, rows packed at the box's width. The buffer is page $F8, in the middle of the start-up code that runs once from build_shifted_pieces and is no longer wanted; the drawing addresses it by L alone, so the box can be no more than 256 bytes.
3. Rub out the old image in the buffer: AND each byte with the complement of the graphic, shifted to the image's x (erase_old_draw_new, erase_aligned_row, erase_row_shifted). This clears every pixel the graphic sets, whatever was under it; there is no mask, and nothing restores what the old image covered.
4. Draw the new image into the buffer: OR (draw_aligned_row, draw_row_shifted). Then, if the object is dissolving, AND its rows with that step's mask (dissolve_sprite).
5. Copy the box back to the screen (the end of dissolve_sprite onwards), clipped to the play area: column 0 is never written, nor anything right of x=$E8 (the fuel gauge's columns).
Graphics are not pre-shifted. An image at x AND 6 = 2, 4 or 6 is shifted as it is drawn, each byte through a pair of 256-byte tables (shift_tables); x is always even. The loops are unrolled, with patched entry points: the row loops are entered so many steps from their ends (erase_row_routines, draw_row_routines), and the copies are LDI chains entered by DJNZs whose displacements are patched (see copy_box_in).
A sprite straddling the left edge of the play area (sprite_left_clip) is handled by taking $60 off both x positions, which moves positions either side of 0 into one unbroken range; only the part of the box right of the edge, at x=8, is copied in and out (copy_in_clipped, copy_out_part_row).
The cost, worked out from the instructions: for a 3-byte-wide sprite 16 rows tall, moved and shifted, a 5-byte box of 17 rows, about 2,500 T-states to copy in, 4,500 to rub out, 4,100 to draw, 2,500 to copy out -- some 13,500 in all, a fifth of a frame.
