D0BD: Draw the landscape
Used by the routine at scroll_landscape.
Called each pass by scroll_landscape, when the view is below the sky (map row 3 or more). The screen shows at most four map tiles, each the size of the play area: the view's page, the one below, the one to the right and the one below that. load_map_window has listed their tile records' addresses after map_window_pointer, which points into the list, and their collision-box lists (37 bytes each) are at loader_entry, ground_cell_bottom_left, ground_cell_top_right and ground_cell_bottom_right; ground_cell says which of the four is being drawn (0 the view's page, 1 below, 2 right, 3 below right). Nothing is scrolled: every piece that can be seen is drawn again, straight onto the screen, from a copy of its graphic pre-shifted for this pass's fine scroll.
First the shift table: the fine scroll (origin_x AND 7) picks one of four tables (at 0, 2, 4 or 6 pixels; shift_table), and the graphic-offset table's start (graphic_offsets, or one byte on when shifted). Then, for each tile: the page-to-screen offsets origin_x and origin_y are moved on to that tile ($E0 to the right, $A0 down, as 8-bit sums) and put back at the end (in after_step_drawn).
A tile record (the tables from tile_records): first byte 0, an empty tile. Otherwise, optionally $99 (the tile is turned upside down: floors become ceilings), optionally $FF (mirrored left-right), optionally a byte of $95 or more (skipped: padding, so a mirrored or upside-down tile can point a few bytes before a plain record and share it), then the number of pieces. Each piece: x and y in the page (pixels; y counts down from the top of the page, and the piece's line rises from there), then a byte whose high nibble is its direction (load_piece_steps) and whose low nibble its number of steps (0 meaning 16), and, only for a flat direction (0 or 7), the codes of its two end pieces (piece_ends). So a tile's ground is a chain of pieces, each a line of steps of one shape: a flat run of 24-pixel steps, a slope up or down, a wall.
Each collision-box list: +0 the number of pieces, then up to seven 5-byte boxes, one per piece: +0 +1 x and y, +2 +3 for a flat piece its width (24 a step) and $14, for a wall $18 and its height (16 a step), for a slope its far end's x and y; +4 its class (piece_classes). These are refilled only while record_ground is set: when the view has moved into a new page this pass (scroll_landscape) or the screen is being built after a restart (map_rebuild); otherwise only the piece count is rewritten.
Each piece is then tested against the view: a piece wholly outside it across (view_x, piece_off_left_edge for the left-hand tiles, piece_off_right_edge for the right) or up and down (view_y, piece_off_top_edge for the upper tiles, piece_off_bottom_edge for the lower) is not drawn. One that may be seen is drawn by the column loop at landscape_step_loop, its attributes coloured by colour_piece, and the next piece taken from next_piece. The renderer uses SP for its POP blitter and reloads it from the fixed address stack_top between pieces.
