D588: Patch the flat-step blitter for the play area's edges and the piece's end caps
Used by the routine at landscape_step_loop.
Flat steps (directions 0 and 7) are three bytes wide and four rows tall, all drawn by blit_flat_step, which has three patchable JRs in its loop and two on its cut path (flat_blit_left_edge, flat_blit_left_rows). This routine resets the loop's three to draw all three columns, then patches them by the column the step starts in (the low five bits of column_address are three less than that column):
  • $1D (start in column 0, the left border): the first JR to flat_blit_left_edge, which skips the first byte and draws columns 1 and 2 (flat_blit_left_two_rows).
  • $1C (start in column 31, one column left of the screen): the same, drawing only the third byte, in column 1 (flat_blit_left_one_row).
  • $18 (columns 27-29): the third JR to flat_blit_left_two, two columns.
  • $19 (columns 28-30): the second JR to flat_blit_one_column, one column.
Then the piece's end caps. A flat piece carries two cap bytes (piece_extra_a for its first step, piece_extra_b for its last, read by piece_ends); bit 4 marks a cap to act on, and bit 7 is set here when it is acted on (landscape_end_pieces looks for $91 and $93). A cap's low two bits pick one of four extra graphics stored after the three variants (offset $24 + 12 * n), drawn in place of the variant. Cap $16 ($96 once seen) draws just the first column, and skips the step if that column is outside. An odd cap marks a drop at that end of the run: if the step is on a byte boundary and the horizontal scroll is moving towards that end, its address and x are kept (first_step_address, piece_kept, scratch_count) for the column fill after the piece (fill_flat_end_column).
The first step of a piece whose start cap is exactly $10 loses its first column (the first JR to flat_blit_lose_first) when the ground is off a byte boundary.
