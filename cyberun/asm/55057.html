D711: Finish a step; at a piece's end repair behind it and colour it; then the next piece and map cell
Used by the routines at blit_right_clip, blit_left_clip, blit_mirrored_left_clip, blit_mirrored_right_clip, blit_one, blit_three, blit_two, blit_mirror_three, blit_mirror_two and blit_mirror_one.
Every step blitter ends with JP after_step_drawn (HL = where the next step starts); the flat-step blitter blit_flat_step stores column_address itself and comes back to the second instruction. edge_column and column_clipped come in further down, at the step counters, for steps handled at the edges.
When a piece's last step is done the stack is put back at stack_top. That address is hard-wired: the landscape is always reached by the play loop's CALL ship_pass (2 bytes), its PUSH IY (2) and the CALL draw_landscape in scroll_landscape (2), below the game's stack top; the fly-in in start_landscape reaches it at the same depth (its CALL, a PUSH BC, the CALL).
Then what lies behind the moving landscape is repaired. The screen is never cleared: the sky is black and the inside of the ground is a $55/$AA checker, and both look the same after a move of two pixels either way, which is why the landscape scrolls in steps of two. Only the strips a moving edge uncovers need painting. Across: when the fine horizontal position comes back to a byte boundary each step has just moved into the next byte column, leaving its old one behind; a sloped piece gets that column filled step by step by fill_under_slope, with the checker ($55AA) or black (0) chosen by the scroll's direction, the piece's direction and whether it is a ceiling (piece_ceiling); a flat piece's drop ends are done by fill_flat_end_column. Up and down: if the landscape moved vertically (scroll_down), fill_behind_moving_piece fills the rows behind every drawn step. Then colour_piece colours the piece.
Then the next piece of this map cell begins (back in draw_landscape); when the cell's pieces are done, the next of the four map cells (ground_cell 0 to 3: upper-left, lower-left, upper-right, lower-right) follows, with the drawing origin moved down a cell (origin_y + $A0) or back up (origin_y + $60, i.e. - $A0), and right a cell (origin_x - $20, i.e. + $E0) for the right-hand pair. After the fourth, origin_x is put back and the landscape is done. draw_landscape jumps to both of those places too.
