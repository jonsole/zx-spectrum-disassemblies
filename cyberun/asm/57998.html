E28E: Start a flat piece that crosses the left-hand side of the play area
The first pass of the flat-piece blitter blit_flat_step comes here when flat_step_clip has patched the JR at the top of its loop to offset $33 (the piece's column is $1C or $1D, that is, it begins just left of the play area, whose columns are 1 to 28). It keeps the column saved for the next piece (column_address) in the right character row, moves L to column 1 of the row, and patches the loop's JR again (offset $4C) so that every later pair of rows goes straight to flat_blit_left_rows's second instruction. Then it skips the graphic's first byte, and through the JR flat_step_clip also patched (0 or 1) maybe a second one, so that only the bytes that lie on the play area are drawn.
Column $1D leaves two bytes of each row to draw (flat_blit_left_two_rows), column $1C one (flat_blit_left_one_row). The POPs that follow are one byte out of step with the graphic's pairs, which is why each row's last write and the drops in those two routines look lopsided.
Input
A the column kept for the next piece, low byte (column_address after the blitter added 3)
HL the screen address of the piece's first byte
