F8E2: Make a flat piece's shifted copies
Used by the routine at build_shifted_pieces.
For build_shifted_pieces, the two flat pieces (directions 0 and 7). A flat piece is three bytes wide and tiles: the ground under it repeats every 24 pixels. So its rows are rotated rather than shifted: the bits that would fall off the right-hand end come back in at the left (the byte that would hold them is cleared and its bits ORed into the row's first byte), and nothing is added to the width. The row pairs alternate as in the blitter blit_flat_step: left to right, then right to left. After each copy, HL' skips 48 bytes: only the first 12 rows of each 28-row copy are made here, the other 16 rows of each having come on the tape already shifted (read from the simulator). The 12 made are the three 4-row variants of the step, the ground that repeats and so can be rotated; the 16 on the tape are the four end caps, already shifted (checked in the simulator for the Graphics page) -- presumably because a cap does not tile, so rotating it would wrap its edge round to the wrong side (assumed).
At the end it patches a byte in the last row of each flat piece's copy shifted by 6 (flat_piece_shifted and flat_piece_two_shifted; to $5C and $40), perhaps to mend a pixel the rotation got wrong (not checked).

HL the piece's header (two bytes: pairs of rows, width)
HL' where the copy shifted by 2 goes; the copies shifted by 4 and 6 follow, 84 bytes apart
