Knight Lore Room structure

The castle

The castle is a grid of 16 by 16 squares, and a room's number is its square's row times 16 plus its column: leaving by the east or west side changes only the low four bits, and north or south adds or takes 16 (screen_west to screen_south). 128 of the 256 squares are rooms. The map below shows each where it lies, as the game draws it on entering; click one for its record.

A room record

A room is a record of a few dozen bytes in location_tbl, and the records are packed end to end with no index: find_screen finds a room by walking them from the start. In order:

ByteWhat
0The room's number.
1The record's length less one: the distance from this byte to the next record.
2Bits 0-2 the ink the whole room is drawn in, always bright on black; bits 3-7 its size, an index into room_size_tbl.
3 ...Its backgrounds, one byte each: indexes into background_type_tbl -- see the scenery.
$FFThe end of the backgrounds, if objects follow. A room with none just ends.
groupsA byte whose bits 3-7 are a template, an index into block_type_tbl (see the templates), and bits 0-2 the number of copies less one; then a position byte per copy.

A position byte is a cell of the room's floor and a level: bits 0-2 the x cell and bits 3-5 the y cell of an 8 by 8 grid, and bits 6-7 the level. next_fg_obj_sprite makes x = $48 + 16 times the x cell, y likewise, and z = the floor height + 12 times the level, then adds the template's offset.

The three sizes, as half-widths about the room's centre at 128, and the floor's height:

SizeHalf xHalf yFloor
06464$80
13264$80
26432$80

A room becomes object records from $5C08 up: the player's two, two for the room's charms, then everything the record names, background pieces first. That leaves 36 records, and six rooms need all 36.
